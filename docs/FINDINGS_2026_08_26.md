## 1. The three-agent training run was a negative result

`results/cb_gnn/counts_rung1_s0.json` — 4500 episodes, 281 updates, 6.8 h.

| arm | success | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| learned | 0.055 | 0.025–0.090 |
| random_vary | 0.060 | 0.030–0.095 |
| greedy_uncertainty | 0.100 | 0.060–0.145 |

Entropy fell 1.609 → 0.678. The policy did not fail to learn; it converged **confidently to
below random**. At three interventions per agent the reward is mostly noise and PPO
committed to whatever the early noise favoured.

## 2. Per-agent budget is the binding constraint, and rung 1 was starved by construction

Budget is *rounds shared by all agents*. Rung 0 gave 8 rounds to 2 agents (4 each); rung 1
gave 9 rounds to 3 agents (**3 each**). Holding total rounds constant across rungs silently
cut per-agent evidence by 25% while each window got harder — rung 1 windows carry 0.94
confounded pairs on average against rung 0's 0.68, and only 25% of windows come free
against 43%.

Greedy, 30 episodes per row:

| config | per agent | per-window identified | joint | settled-wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rung 0, budget 8 | 4 | 0.250 | 0.100 | 35% |
| rung 0, budget 32 | 16 | 0.650 | 0.533 | 22% |
| rung 1, budget 9 | 3 | 0.200 | 0.067 | 41% |
| rung 1, budget 36 | 12 | 0.467 | 0.300 | 18% |

Both rungs were trained on the steep part of this curve, which is why every arm bunched
near the floor and rung 0's learned-vs-greedy margin was 7pp with overlapping CIs.

## 3. What the belief engine is actually wrong about

Census over 40 episodes / 120 windows / 1236 claims (budget 12, n_int 250, B 12):

```
45 confident errors (3.6% of claims)
  missed a real edge     22   (49%)
  confounding misread    17   (38%)
  invented an edge        4   ( 9%)
  direction wrong         2   ( 4%)
bootstrap agreement with the wrong answer:  0.8 → 14,  0.9 → 12,  1.0 → 19
```

The engine is **under-powered, not incorrect**. Errors are unanimous across resamples,
which rules out Monte-Carlo noise but is exactly what an under-powered test produces: the
same mistake in every replicate.

### Bootstrap count and data volume trade against each other

| spend | identified | confidently wrong | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| B=5, n_int=250 | 0.167 | 0.483 | 1.4 s/ep |
| B=12, n_int=250 (baseline) | 0.233 | 0.350 | 3.6 s/ep |
| B=25, n_int=250 | 0.267 | 0.283 | 6.6 s/ep |
| B=12, n_int=1000 | **0.350** | **0.250** | 6.7 s/ep |

**For equal compute, data beats resamples** — B=25 and n_int=1000 both cost ~2×; the first
buys +3.4pp, the second +11.7pp. Spend on `n_int`, not `cb_n_boot`.

B=5 is not viable: it converts *unsure* into *wrong* (false confidence), because at five
resamples the 0.7 bar quantises to 4-of-5.

### The significance threshold is already right

| alpha | identified | type errors |
|---|---|---|
| 0.01 (current) | **0.311** | 16 |
| 0.05 | 0.056 | 23 |
| 0.10 | 0.022 | 32 |

Loosening alpha does recover missed edges (16 → 9) but collapses identification, because
alpha gates the interventional orientation channels as well as the skeleton. Type errors
double. **Keep 0.01.** Raising the claim bar is also not a lever — errors sit at 0.9–1.0
agreement, above every bar tested.

Open idea, untested: *decouple* the two uses of alpha — a looser threshold for skeleton
search, a strict one for orientation evidence — since their error costs are opposite.

## 4. The deterministic (infinite-data) environment

Belief as a **version space**: the set of MAGs still consistent with what is established.
An intervention prunes it to those agreeing with the truth about the intervened node's
ancestry. A claim is *resolved* when all survivors agree — and since the truth never leaves
the set, resolved implies resolved correctly. Settled-wrong cannot occur.

Scaling works because Markov-equivalent MAGs share adjacencies, so the class varies only in
the marks on the true skeleton: 3^(edges) candidates rather than 4^(pairs). Verified
against exhaustive enumeration at k=4 — identical membership, 10× faster. (Where they
differed, the exhaustive set was wrong: it admitted non-maximal ancestral graphs.)

### Single-agent play has exactly zero headroom

Bayes-optimal adaptive DP versus myopic greedy, exact, 15 graphs: **optimal − greedy =
0.000 on 15 of 15**. With infinite data the objective is submodular and myopic selection is
optimal. There is nothing to learn in single-window experiment selection.

### Coordination headroom is real, on the metric that is reported

Against a clairvoyant optimising for identification, 40 worlds per row, k=4, 3 shared
nodes, one intervention per agent:

| agents | greedy | best possible | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.463 | 0.500 | +0.037 ± 0.021 (+8%) |
| 3 | 0.475 | 0.692 | +0.217 ± 0.033 (+46%) |
| **4** | 0.406 | 0.713 | **+0.306 ± 0.040 (+75%)** |
| 6 | 0.729 | 0.929 | +0.200 ± 0.038 (+27%) |
| 8 | 0.872 | 0.984 | +0.113 ± 0.023 (+13%) |
| 10 | 0.922 | 0.995 | +0.072 ± 0.019 (+8%) |

Three structural facts:

- **The headroom is entirely coordination** — single-agent headroom is exactly zero, so
  every point above comes from agents dividing contested variables.
- **It lives in a band**, peaking where interventions ≈ contested variables and closing on
  both sides. Outside the band an experiment measures nothing.
- **Greedy degrades through the band while the bound improves** (0.463 → 0.475 → 0.406
  against 0.500 → 0.692 → 0.713) — the divergence the thesis needs.

Measured in *claim counts* the same gap is only 2–5%, which is why an earlier pass rejected
this environment. Identification is all-or-nothing, so the claims greedy misses are
disproportionately the last ones a window needs. **Report identification, not claims.**

### Practical ceiling: window density, not graph size

Cost is 3^(edges in a window), so global graphs to d=23 are cheap. At k=7 with 6 agents the
density guard rejected 159 worlds to yield 10 — every other agent's private variable is a
potential latent confounder, so windows fill in with bidirected edges. **k ≤ 6.**

## 5. Design implication: two layers

| layer | environment | answers |
|---|---|---|
| coordination | deterministic version space | can agents learn to divide experiments? |
| robustness | statistical (current) | does it survive finite data? |

The coordination layer runs in milliseconds and has a **computable optimum**, so results
report *fraction of achievable headroom closed* rather than a margin over a baseline with
overlapping CIs. Neither layer alone supports the thesis.

An unresolved tension the roadmap must address: the coordination band wants **few**
interventions per agent, while statistical power wants **many**. The resolution suggested by
§3 is to buy evidence with `n_int` (rows per experiment) rather than with more rounds.

## 6. Tooling built

- `scripts/trace_episode.py` — dumps every step of an episode: who intervened on what, and
  every claim's outcome plus the frequency behind it.
- `scripts/trace_view.py` — renders traces as a step-through page.
- `cb/claims.py::enumerate_claims` — per-claim detail; `score_window` is now a tally over
  it, so a trace and the reward cannot disagree about what a claim is.
- W&B logging on every run, offline by default (`--no_wandb` to disable).

## 7. Retractions

Stated confidently, then falsified by better measurement. Do not cite these.

1. *"Coordination is worth ~0.9 claims, 12/12 worlds."* That compared decentralised greedy
   against **centralised greedy** — two heuristics, neither optimal. At 8+ agents the
   "coordinated" one is reliably worse (−1.25 ± 0.30, losing 19 of 20), because summing
   gains over all windows over-values shared variables. It never measured headroom.
2. *"Coordination gaps of +1.8 and +2.8."* n=10, where that estimator's standard error is
   0.5–1.0. At n=20 the same configurations gave +0.50 ± 0.42 and −1.25 ± 0.30.
3. *"Most confidently-wrong claims are resampling noise."* True only of the marginal
   population; at B=12 the residual is dominated by unanimous errors that B cannot reach.
4. *"The deterministic environment is rejected — greedy solves it at every scale."*
   Measured in claim counts, which is the wrong metric. See §4.
5. *"11 of 12 confident errors are adjacency, so the skeleton is the whole story."* A
   12-claim sample. The 45-error census puts adjacency at 58%, confounding at 38%.
6. *"Rung 1 take 1 was killed at update ~220."* It ran to completion; the duplicate
   launched on that assumption was the process later stopped.
