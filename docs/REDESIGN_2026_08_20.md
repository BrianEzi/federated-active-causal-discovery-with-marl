## Directive 1 — Turn-taking, one intervention per step

**What changes.** Simultaneous action is replaced by turn-taking: exactly one agent
intervenes per round. Agent selection by **round-robin** or **random**; both to be
implemented and compared, since the choice is itself a design variable that has never been
swept.

**Provenance, stated honestly:** simultaneous action was the student's own suggestion, made
without consulting the supervisor. It should be reported as a design choice that was tried
and superseded, not quietly dropped — it produced a real finding (see below).

**What it invalidates.**

- **Every two-agent number is protocol-specific.** The 10-seed result (learned median 0.387
  against a random floor of 0.197) is a *simultaneous-action* result. It does not transfer.
- **GATE 2's finding dissolves by construction.** Our result was that greedy agents fail
  because they systematically converge on the same unique best target — 0 of 74 collisions
  involved a tie for either agent. Under turn-taking **collisions cannot occur**, because
  only one agent moves. The finding remains true and reportable *about simultaneous action*,
  and it becomes the justification for turn-taking rather than a loose end.
- **Greedy probably returns as a real baseline** — and per the supervisor this is *good*: a
  stronger opponent makes beating it a stronger claim. Expect GATE 2 to pass under
  turn-taking. That must be re-measured, not assumed.
- **Budget semantics change.** "Budget 8" currently means 8 interventions *each*. Under
  turn-taking it has to be redefined as a total across the system, or as turns per agent.
  Whichever is chosen, it is not comparable to the current numbers.

## Directive 2 — Clamp becomes the only mode (student's proposal, accepted here)

**The argument, and it follows from a measurement made today.** A constant hard intervention
recovers **93–98%** of the information a varying one does for learning your own structure
(measured at d=4 and d=5; see `docs/SA_EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`). But only clamping cuts
confounding for a partner — varying is useless there (rescue 0.000 at scale 2.0 and 1.0).

So clamp gets ~95% of the private benefit **and** all of the social benefit. It **weakly
dominates** vary, and under turn-taking — where a move spent is a move the other agent does
not get — dominance is decisive. Collapsing the action space to "which node to clamp"
removes a dimension without removing the coordination problem, which becomes one of
*targeting and timing* rather than of mode.

**MUST BE VERIFIED BEFORE ADOPTION, NOT INHERITED.** The 93–98% figure is a **single-agent**
measurement on a pooled dataset. The two-agent setting differs in ways that could change it:
the regime split (clean/dirty) means the clamped rows are scored *separately*, and the
pooling argument that explains the small gap may not survive that. If clamped rows are
scored alone, the collinearity objection that we just retired could come back.

The standing lesson applies exactly here — *a metric verified on unrepresentative data
proves nothing about the case you care about*. Re-measure clamp-vs-vary inside `ma/env2.py`
under the regime split before deleting the vary action.

## Directive 3 — Scale: toward 20–30 nodes and ~5 agents, and vary the topology

The supervisor's framing: push scale as far as results hold. 30+ nodes is the natural
stopping point in the literature; 5 agents would be valuable; comparing topologies is
worthwhile in itself.

**The federation is what makes this possible.** Window size binds, not global `d`. Five
agents × 5 private + 5 shared = **30 nodes with a window of 10**, and the subset DP at
`3^10 = 59k` is comfortable.

**The binding constraint is the confounding enumeration, `3^(shared pairs)`:**

| shared \|X\| | pairs | full `3^p` | **≤2 confounded** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 10 | 59,049 | **201** |
| 6 | 15 | 1.4×10⁷ | **451** |
| 8 | 28 | 2.3×10¹³ | **1,569** |
| 10 | 45 | 3.0×10²¹ | **4,051** |

**The proposed fix: truncate by sparsity.** Confounding is rare — measured mean ≈0.50
bidirected pairs per episode at T1. Enumerating only assignments with **at most `m`**
simultaneously-confounded pairs costs `sum_{j<=m} C(pairs, j) · 2^j`, which takes `|X|=6`
from 14 million to **451**. It is a principled truncation: the dropped mass can be bounded
rather than hoped away, and `m` becomes a swept parameter with a stated error budget.

**Two honest caveats.**

1. **The confinement proof is a TWO-AGENT result.** "Confounding is confined to the shared
   set" was proved and exhaustively verified for two agents. With five agents and overlapping
   shared sets, a third agent's private node may confound a pair visible to a fourth. **The
   proof must be redone and may fail.** If it fails, the belief needs full MAG machinery and
   the score stops decomposing — which would be the single biggest threat to the scaling
   plan. Establish this **before** building anything on top of it.
2. Beyond `d ≈ 9` per window the subset DP itself stops. Thirty nodes works only because each
   window stays small; it is not a global-inference claim and must not be written as one.

## Directive 4 — Percolation, and the graph-density defect it exposes

The supervisor mentioned percolation theory in passing. The actionable reading:

**`prior_p = 0.5` does not scale, and it is now a blocking defect.** The literature's sparse
regime is `p_e = Θ(1/d)` (Chevalley, Mehrjou & Schwab, arXiv:2511.02536, verified). In
`G(d, p)` the giant component appears at `p_c = 1/d`, so that regime *is* percolation-critical
scaling.

| d | our fixed p=0.5 → E[degree] | literature ER-2 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2.0 | 2 |
| 10 | 4.5 | 2 |
| 30 | **14.5** | 2–4 |

At `d = 4–5` our fixed 0.5 lands in the sparse regime **by accident**. At `d = 30` it is 15×
above the percolation threshold — one connected blob, where structure recovery is neither
realistic nor informative.

**Action:** `prior_p` becomes a function of `d`, approximately `2/(d-1)`, and the change is
swept rather than asserted. It is already on the parameter-audit list; this promotes it to
blocking for any scaling claim.

Cite Chevalley et al. for the scaling regime. The percolation connection is **our framing**
— state it as such, do not attribute it.

## Consequences for the plan

**Dissertation due 8 September. Experiments were to close 27 August.**

The student's position, recorded: the timeline is tight but achievable with sustained work.
Recorded also is the assistant's concern, which has not been withdrawn — turn-taking
invalidates the current two-agent protocol, and 5 agents at 30 nodes requires the sparse
assignment scheme, a multi-agent topology, and a re-proof of confinement. Both the "two-agent

plausible; doing both fully in the remaining window is not obviously possible.

**Suggested ordering, by dependency rather than by appeal:**

1. **Re-prove confinement for `n` agents.** Cheap (it is a structural enumeration, like the
   original), and everything else depends on the answer. If it fails, the whole scaling plan
   changes shape — better to know in a day than in a week.
2. **Turn-taking + clamp-only**, with round-robin and random selection. Re-run the gates.
   Expect GATE 2 to pass; expect the two-agent headline to need re-earning.
3. **Sparse assignments with a bounded truncation error**, plus `prior_p = f(d)`.
4. **Scale the ladder:** 2 agents → 3 → 5, and nodes upward as the window allows. Stop where
   results stop holding, and *report where that is* — the stopping point is a result.
5. **Topology comparison** if time allows; it is the most expendable item, and the honest
   thing is to say so in advance rather than drop it silently.

**What is already banked and does not need redoing:** the single-agent result, the belief
machinery and its validation chain (1e-10, 1e-13, 62×), the metric correction and its
reachability guard, the GATE 2 mechanism finding (as a simultaneous-action result), and the
clamp-vs-vary measurement.
