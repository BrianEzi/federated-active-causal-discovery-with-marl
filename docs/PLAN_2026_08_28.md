## 2. The five open findings

### F1 — the iso-budget curve is normalised wrongly *(ours)*

**The attack.** "You held moves-per-node fixed, but your required cover is sublinear in k, so
you handed the big windows a more generous budget."

It lands. Under oracle evidence the belief is a deterministic function of the SET of
intervened nodes, and `_apply_ancestry` makes the required set forced: a directed edge needs
its TAIL, a bidirected pair needs BOTH endpoints. Measured, that set is `0.757k` at k=4 and
`0.542k` at k=30 — so a budget of `0.5k` covers 66% of the requirement at k=4 and 92% at k=30.

**Fixes, cheapest first.**
1. Plot joint success against `budget / required_cover`, one curve per k. If the curves
   collapse onto each other, scale-agnosticism holds in a form the objection cannot reach.
   Eval-only on existing checkpoints.
2. Add the **oracle-cover planner** as an arm — it intervenes on exactly the forced set. Free
   to build, and it converts every comparison into learned vs heuristic vs optimal.
3. Train at need-matched budgets on 3–4 rungs.

**Live warning.** `w20iso` (k=20 trained at budget 40) is at update 70 with entropy 3.042
against a maximum of 3.045; its starved twin was at 2.369 by update 60. Making the task easier
may be making it harder to learn — at ~94% achievable success the reward stops discriminating.
If that holds, need-matched runs must target ~50% success, not the most generous budget.

### F2 — the coordination claim rested on an uncoordinated baseline *(ours)*

**The attack.** "Your policy is trained on the joint criterion and your baseline optimises a
per-agent one. Of course it duplicates less — your baseline wasn't trying."

`PartitionedGreedyAgent` is the control: the same myopic rule with the shared surface split by
a positional convention every agent computes alone, plus a tie-break towards the least
already-touched node read from the DISCLOSED partner counts — the same channel the learned
policy sees. No learning, no communication beyond what the environment already broadcasts.

**Caution for whoever reads the first numbers.** The first version of this class did not
coordinate at all: duplicate coverage 0.167 against the plain rule's 0.169 at k=6, and *worse*
than plain at k=12 (0.150 vs 0.126), because with four agents on four shared nodes each owns
one, settles it in a move, and spends the rest of the episode in the fallback. Any
head-to-head taken before commit `HEAD` is void. The test now pins a material margin rather
than a bare inequality.

**Also.** Promote duplicate coverage to a primary reported metric — it is the mechanism, and
unlike the joint rate it is not confounded by the objective mismatch. And report the cost: at
k=20 the learned policy leaves NOBODY finished 54% of the time against greedy's 32%.

### F3 — the agent axis never trained *(theirs)*

Your share of the interventions touching your own window falls as 1/n and I(S;A)/H tracks it:
0.723, 0.574, 0.067, 0.035 at 2, 3, 6, 8 agents. Greedy meanwhile does *better* with more
agents (0.934 per-window at 8 against 0.886 at 4), so the task is not harder — the learning
signal is diluted.

**The control that names it.** 8 agents, k=6, budget 24, all held fixed; change only the
private/shared split — 4 private + 2 shared against 2 private + 4 shared. Same window size,
same agent count, same budget, less of your window disturbed by partners. If MI recovers at 4
private, dilution is confirmed and the negative becomes a MARL result rather than a
convergence failure.

Whatever it shows, report the agent axis as an OPTIMISATION limit with the MI evidence
attached, and claim nothing about coordination at 6 or 8 agents.

### F4 — the evaluation protocol is not tight enough to quote *(ours)*

`IndependentPPO.__init__` calls `torch.manual_seed(config.seed)`, so every evaluation of a
loaded policy replays ONE fixed sample path and the quoted standard errors never included
policy stochasticity. On top of that the learned arm is genuinely more graph-sensitive than
the rule: at k=20, per-window moved 0.275 → 0.169 across two seed bases while greedy moved
0.285 → 0.308.

- **Argmax as primary.** It is both stronger and far more stable (joint 0.100/0.108 across
  bases where sampling gave 0.058/0.017). Sampling was quietly handicapping the learned arm.
- **Three seed bases x 200 paired episodes, three training seeds** on every headline cell.
- **The MI check is a gate**, not an option. Run it before quoting any learned number.

### F5 — everything is measured in an idealisation *(theirs)*

Oracle skeleton, oracle evidence, scale-free only. Replicate one rung under sampled evidence
and one under Erdős–Rényi. Without it the thesis claims scaling in an idealisation, which is a
much smaller claim than the one we want.

### F6 — GRPO: mostly refuted for THIS environment, kept as a future-paper thread *(ours)*

The student proposed replacing the critic with a group-relative baseline, on the reasoning
that agents are graded on actions they did not take. The diagnosis is right and is being fixed
by the other agent (transition masking). GRPO as the remedy is a different question, and the
cheap test answered it without training anything (`grpo_signal.py`, 60 states x 10 rollouts).

**The objection, written before the numbers.** Every rollout in a group starts from the same
state, so the group mean is a CONSTANT -- structurally the same object as V(s). A constant
cannot reorder advantages within a group, so vanilla GRPO cannot do credit assignment; it can
only swap a badly-fit critic for a well-estimated empirical baseline. Confirmed empirically:
the group-baselined and critic-baselined advantages give identical within-group correlations
to three decimals at 2, 6 and 8 agents.

| agents | within-group share of return variance | critic V vs true group mean | r(return, own causal contribution) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9.3% | 0.733 | **−0.247 ± 0.076** |
| 3 | 25.1% | 0.617 | −0.115 ± 0.041 |
| 6 | **82.3%** | 0.261 | 0.165 ± 0.018 |
| 8 | **75.9%** | 0.334 | **0.074 ± 0.017** |

1. At 6–8 agents roughly 80% of return variance is WITHIN group, which no state baseline can
   touch. Vanilla GRPO's ceiling is low exactly where the problem is.
2. The critic really is badly fit at scale (0.26–0.33 against 0.73 at two agents), so the
   student's instinct about the value head is right — it just does not matter much given (1).
3. **The reward barely tracks the agent's own causal contribution at all** (r = 0.074 at 8
   agents). That is a property of the REWARD, not of the baseline, and no baseline scheme
   rescues it.

**Do not spend pre-freeze compute on a GRPO training race.** On this evidence a win would most
likely be variance reduction wearing a credit-assignment costume, and we would be reporting a
novel mechanism we had not demonstrated.

**What stays on the agenda, background priority, for a future paper.**
- **Counterfactual groups.** Hold partners' action sequences fixed across the group and vary
  only this agent's. The group baseline then stops being a state baseline and becomes a
  difference-reward estimator, which genuinely does credit assignment and stays decentralised.
- **The catch, and it is decisive for this environment.** Under oracle evidence the belief is
  a deterministic function of the intervened set, so the difference reward is computable
  EXACTLY and for free. Estimating it by sampling is strictly worse. GRPO's novelty here is
  undercut by our own determinism.
- **Where it would genuinely be novel: the SAMPLED-evidence engine.** There the belief is not
  deterministic, the counterfactual cannot be computed, and group-relative sampling is the
  natural estimator. That is the version worth a paper, and it composes with F5.
