The problem

Why this is not just causal discovery with more computers

Several organisations hold different slices of one system. Each sees its own private variables plus a shared set everyone observes. Each can run experiments, but only on what it can see, and none will hand over its raw data.

Two things follow that don't arise with a single agent. Experiments are contended — two agents testing the same shared variable have wasted a round between them. And every agent's private variables are invisible confounders for everyone else: they create correlations in your window that you cannot explain, because their cause is behind someone else's wall.

The question this branch set out to answer is whether agents can learn to divide that work better than a sensible rule of thumb, without sharing data.

How we got here

The route, and why each turn was taken

The results were real but the setting was broken

The grading exempted a whole class of question on a false argument about what observation can determine. Removing the exemption made the task harder — and exposed that the budget made 25 of 40 episodes unsolvable by any assignment whatsoever. Every earlier comparison was partly scored on impossible episodes. Budget is now set so the ceiling is 100%.

Agents could not remember what their partners had done

Partners announce their moves each round, but the policy is feedforward and cannot retain an announcement. We added cumulative per-partner counts — converting disclosure into memory without disclosing anything new.

A policy was welded to one exact configuration

Nothing in the network's learned weights depended on window size; two bookkeeping details did. Fixing them made one checkpoint run at four different window sizes and agent counts — the prerequisite for any claim about generality.

The graph generator was a leftover

Erdős–Rényi came from a Bayesian method we no longer use. Moving to scale-free — at matched density — gives 5.3× as many hidden-common-cause signatures, the exact structure this thesis is about. It was incidental before and is routine now.

The target changed: from “what” to “whose”

A confounded pair used to be recorded as “something unobserved links these two”, full stop. It is now a named claim: which group of your variables one hidden cause explains, and which agent owns it. This is a question single-agent causal discovery cannot even pose.

And that exposed something uncomfortable

The old idealised environment could not see a partner's private experiment at all — not under-rewarded, invisible. So its headline number is about dividing shared work and contains no cooperation, and never could have.

Scale was blocked by the wrong suspect

We assumed the belief representation. Profiling said otherwise: the ground-truth computation was searching every subset of variables. Replaced with a polynomial algorithm, validated at 1440/1440 exact agreement. That, plus a pairwise belief, took the ceiling from 6 variables to 30.

Result two · the new contribution

Naming whose hidden variable it is

Two facts make this well-posed. A hidden cause of two shared variables must live in exactly one named agent's private block — so it's a choice among a handful of suspects, not a search over arbitrary structures. And “one hidden cause of three things” versus “three separate hidden causes” is observationally indistinguishable but interventionally separable — by an experiment only the owner can run.

And the agents find it themselves. Three seeds, all arms on identical episodes: learned 0.670 ± 0.005 against the theory-derived schedule's 0.667 and greedy's 0.582. They discovered a strategy as good as the one we derived — without being told it, and while being paid only for their own window.

Result three

Transfer to noisy data fails — and we know why

The hope was to train cheaply in the idealised setting and carry the policy into realistic, finite data. It does not work, and the interesting part is the mechanism.

In the environment it trained on

0.988

Problems solved, with only 4% of shared work duplicated. Near-perfect division of labour.

Moved to noisy data

0.171

Below random (0.208) and below greedy (0.229). Not confusion — a confused policy drifts toward random, not past it.

A hypothesis we killed, worth mentioning because it nearly cost a day. The first explanation was that the idealisation teaches agents never to repeat an experiment, while repeating buys statistical power on real data. A sweep refuted it: dividing the work is better in both environments, in identical order. We built the test before the fix.

Result four · today

From six variables to thirty

The belief used to hold an explicit list of every possible structure — exact, and growing as 3 to the power of the connections. At 30 variables that is about 1019 entries. Two changes removed the wall.

The wall was not where we assumed. After building the pairwise belief it still died at 15 variables. Profiling found the cost in the ground-truth computation used to grade the agents, which was searching every subset of variables — 2ⁿ per pair. Swapped for a polynomial algorithm and checked against the old one on 1,440 cases: 1440/1440 identical.

And the new belief is a conservative approximation, not a different answer: across 1,920 pairs it is sometimes less decisive than the exact version and never once contradicts it.

First trained result at scale, an hour old. 8-variable windows, 20 nodes, 150 episodes: learned 0.698 ± 0.027 against greedy's 0.605, and more than double on the all-agents-correct measure (0.413 vs 0.193). One seed.

The honest cost. The exact “best possible score” is computed by enumeration, so it exists only below 6 variables. Above that we have honest bounds. The write-up should say exactly that: exact headroom at small scale, bounds above.

What we are not claiming

  • That individual agents are altruistic. Testing your own private variable also helps you, so budget share doesn't separate the motives. The reward ablation is running.
  • That the headline is generator-independent. The 2.3× result has only been measured on one graph type. Resolving that is the top queued job.
  • That the noise dial fixes transfer. The machinery works at three noise levels; whether it helps is unmeasured.
  • That attribution scales. It exists at three agents. Its cost grows with the number of partners, and the pairwise belief does not help with that — a pairwise attribution is the missing piece.

Corrections made against ourselves

Six claims were withdrawn over two days, four of them for the same reason: a difference reported that was smaller than its own noise. Two process changes followed — every comparison is now paired on identical episodes, and nothing is reported below 150 episodes. The withdrawals are recorded in the findings document alongside the results.
