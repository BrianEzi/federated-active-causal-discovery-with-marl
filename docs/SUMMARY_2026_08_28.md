**Two corrections happened in sequence and both stand. Neither cancels the other.** The

## 1. The two corrections, in order

### 1a. The greedy baseline was handicapped

`UncertaintyGreedyAgent` takes `bar=0.7`, and **every** construction in the repository used
that default, while the deterministic, factored and attributed backends all grade at
`claim_bar=1.0`. Greedy treated a claim that 70% of surviving hypotheses agreed on as
settled and stopped scoring it, and forfeited the round when nothing cleared its own bar —
while the environment still counted that claim open.

Worth **+0.233** to greedy at four agents on scale-free, **+0.007** on Erdős–Rényi. Enough
to invert the attribution headline. Greedy at 1.0 is still truth-free: it reads only its own
belief frequencies, at the threshold the grading uses.

Two possible objections were tested and neither holds:

* **Greedy plays a different game.** `ma/attribution_greedy.py` scores unsure latent GROUPS
  as well as structure. It is *worse* than plain greedy (0.784 vs 0.824): every bidirected
  edge joins two SHARED nodes, so the attribution term steers greedy off the private probes
  whose structural pruning was resolving attribution anyway. **Attribution is better served
  by resolving structure than by targeting confounded pairs directly.**
* **The learner is scored stochastically while baselines play their argmax.** Scoring the
  learner at its argmax is *worse* (0.613 vs 0.733), and the best logit temperature does not
  survive held-out validation (+0.029 on the set it was chosen from, +0.009 on fresh
  episodes — selection bias across six candidates).

### 1b. The policies had not converged

The 4,000-episode policies barely conditioned on their observation. Mutual information
between observation and action, I(S;A)/H: **0.035 / 0.188 / 0.291** against greedy's 1.000
by construction. One agent was very nearly a fixed mixture. Final training entropy ~1.05,
where converged runs on this codebase sit at 0.30–0.90.

That single fact explains the whole shape: a bad argmax (the mode of a state-independent
mixture is one action repeated), a useless temperature dial (sharpening a fixed mixture only
makes it a fixed action), and a reward ablation that moved private share by 0.005 because it
was retuning mixture weights rather than a decision rule.

At 20,000 episodes, four measurements move together:

| | 4,000 episodes | 20,000 episodes |
|---|---|---|
| final entropy | ~1.05 | **0.670** |
| I(S;A)/H | 0.035 / 0.188 / 0.291 | **0.481 / 0.339 / 0.599 / 0.498** |
| vs fair greedy | −0.257 ± 0.030 | **−0.060 ± 0.021** |
| vs theory schedule | −0.027 ± 0.044 | **+0.170 ± 0.037** |

The mutual information now matches the other session's **trained** window ladder
(0.513–0.616) against their untrained 8-agent arm's 0.034.

## 3. Final results

All paired per episode on identical episodes, greedy at the graded bar, 150 evaluation
episodes per arm, policies trained to 20,000 episodes.

### Attribution

| | vs fair greedy | vs theory schedule |
|---|---|---|
| **3 agents** (3 seeds) | **−0.007 ± 0.006** | **+0.150 ± 0.024** |
| **4 agents** (2 seeds) | **−0.064 ± 0.004** | **+0.153 ± 0.018** |

Per-seed: 3 agents −0.018 / +0.002 / −0.004; 4 agents −0.068 / −0.060. The two 4-agent
seeds agree to 0.008, so that gap is a property of the configuration, not seed noise.

**The margin over the hand-designed schedule is identical at both sizes (+0.150, +0.153)
while the standing against greedy degrades with agent count.** The learner scales relative
to the theory-derived reference and loses ground only against the adaptive one.

### The noise dial (`version_space`, sampled evidence, 6 runs)

| n_int | learned | greedy@1.0 | pooled paired margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.497 | 0.443 | **+0.053 ± 0.022** |
| 1000 | 0.747 | 0.647 | **+0.100 ± 0.025** |
| 4000 | 0.863 | 0.740 | **+0.123 ± 0.025** |

All six positive, and **the margin grows monotonically with data quality**. The cleaner the
belief signal, the more a learned policy can exploit that a myopic rule cannot.

### The generator confound (job 5, 4 agents, 20,000 episodes, 3 seeds each)

| generator | learned | greedy@0.7 | greedy@1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| scale-free | 0.956 ± 0.010 | 0.562 | 0.813 |
| Erdős–Rényi | 0.911 ± 0.031 | 0.436 | 0.407 |

**The confound was episode count, not the generator.** The scale-free run that appeared to
lose to greedy was measured at 3,000 episodes; at 20,000 it is the best arm in the table.
The generator does move one arm and it is the **baseline** — greedy is 4.2 SE weaker on
Erdős–Rényi while the learner is statistically identical across the two.

### Structural limits

* **Attribution cost is exponential in the PARTNER count, not window density.** The edge
  distribution is near-identical at 3, 4 and 6 agents (≥8 edges: 0.268 / 0.263 / 0.208), so
  density cannot explain a 34 s probe at three agents against >3000 s at four. An
  attribution is a clique partition × a choice of owner, and owners are partners.
* `ma/density_guard.py` (`--max_edges 7`) rescues four agents — median reset 0.73 s, max
  3.22 s, against an unbounded tail — at the cost of ~26% of confounded draws. **Six agents
  does not come back**; the cap that would reach it rejects 79%.
* The guard removes nearly the same slice at every agent count, which is why the scale
  comparison survives it. `attr3a_guarded` vs `attr3a_peragent` measures the distortion:
  probe_then_work −0.002, greedy +0.031, random +0.049 — it compresses the range from the
  bottom and leaves the strong policy untouched.

### Job 2 — the reward ablation

Shared reward beats per-agent by **−0.080 ± 0.019** (3 seeds, paired). A shared reward is
one scalar at episode end: it moves no private data and does not breach the federated
constraint, so it can simply be adopted. What the result costs is the *bonus* claim that
coordination appears without being paid for — never the thesis itself.

Note the private-move share is 0.748 (per-agent) against 0.743 (shared), essentially
identical, so the shared reward did **not** work by making agents probe privately more often.

### Job 3 — private sets of three

Attribution accuracy was predicted to FALL with private-set size. It **rose** (0.808 →
0.841, two seeds agreeing to ±0.001) and structure rose too. The mechanism the job was
designed to isolate did not appear.

## 4. Turn-aware credit — implemented, and RETRACTED as an explanation

Under turn-taking, `env.step` forces every agent except the active one to pass and discards
its move, but `collect` stored a transition for **every** agent **every** round — pairing an
action that never executed with a reward a partner caused. At *n* agents that is (n−1)/n of
the batch.

`PPOConfig.turn_aware_credit` (default **OFF**) records one transition per agent per TURN,
with rewards arriving while an agent is inactive accrued forward onto its most recent
action. Validated before use: flag off is byte-identical; rows per agent fall 45 → 11–12 at
four agents; the reward difference is accounted to 1e-6 as exactly the reward preceding each
agent's first turn, which a policy gradient must drop.

**It does not fix eight agents.** Full 4,000-episode matched run:

| | baseline | turn-aware |
|---|---|---|
| final entropy | 1.814 | **1.666** |
| learned success | 0.100 | **0.107** |
| greedy | 0.480 | 0.480 |

The optimisation genuinely improves and the gap widens through training (−0.042 at update 10
to −0.148 at 249). Performance does not move. **I proposed this as the root cause of the
agent-count collapse; the direct test does not support that.** The fix stays on correctness
grounds — gradients computed on discarded actions are wrong however little they cost — but
it is not the answer.

## 5. How this meets the other session's free-riding finding

Their measurement (`21a79ac`) is the more likely account of the agent-count collapse, and it
is **distinct from** the phantom-transition bug rather than a restatement of it:

> At every agent count an agent's return tracks its partners' causal contribution more
> closely than its own — negatively at two and three agents (−0.247), so the gradient points
> away from contributing. The reward is a function of the STATE of the window, not of who
> moved it.

Masking phantom transitions corrects **which** transitions receive gradient. It does nothing
about the identification bonus landing on an agent regardless of who caused it. Their
`difference_reward` (`4b7696a`) addresses the second, and matched runs are in flight with
the MI gate — not the score — as the decider. **That is the live hypothesis for eight
agents; mine is eliminated.**

Eliminated so far for the 8-agent collapse: undertraining (their `a08long`, 16,000 episodes
→ entropy 1.437), lower entropy coefficient, a shared trunk, and turn-aware credit.

## 7. Process failures worth keeping

Every significant correction on 27 August came from the student asking a skeptical question,
not from this session volunteering it. Recorded as a standing instruction.

* **Trend claims from partial training are inadmissible, not merely caveated.** Three times
  in two days a curve's shape was read off an unfinished run — the entropy "on track to be
  sharper", and "the dial saturates" twice, which was wrong in both senses once the runs
  completed.
* **Build a comparison from the target run's own config, never a neighbour's.** Twice in one
  night: `--n_obs 60` where the dial used 1000, and `gnn` where the ladder used
  `gnn_portable`. Both produced plausible-looking numbers. `window_rate` at update 0 is a
  better identity check than entropy, which is 1.946 for any k=6 policy at initialisation.
* **Verify the artefact, not the exit status.** A scripted `str.replace` failed silently
  while the file still parsed and the helper functions sat unused; two output pipes buffered
  behind `grep`/`tail`.
* **An arm that scores badly must be checked for having TRAINED before it is interpreted.**
  Entropy near maximum and near-zero mutual information are the signature, both are free.
* **`ma/evaluate.py::_claims_success` is still wrong** for the attributed backend: it calls
  `score_window` with defaults, so it scores the superseded criterion and ignores
  attribution. Training and every number from `attr_score.py` are unaffected — but the
  `success` field in `results/attr_scale/*.json` must not be quoted.
