## 2. What the project is now, and what changed on 26–27 August

MSc thesis: federated active causal discovery with multi-agent RL. Several agents each see
a slice of one causal system (their own private variables plus a shared set), can intervene
only on what they see, spend a shared budget of rounds, and must recover the causal
structure of their own window. The question is whether they LEARN to coordinate experiments
better than a sensible rule, without sharing private data.

**THE TARGET CHANGED ON 26 AUGUST and this is the thing to understand before anything
else.** Agents used to be graded on recovering their window's MAG, in which a hidden common
cause is an anonymous bidirected edge. They are now graded on **attribution**: which
*clique* of their variables one hidden cause explains, and **which agent owns it**.
Attribution REPLACES the confounding claim; the two are never scored together.

Read `cb/attribution.py`'s module docstring. The three facts that make it work:

1. **Every bidirected edge in a window joins two SHARED variables, and its latent lies in
   exactly one agent's private block.** Follows from the edge rule. So attribution is a
   choice among NAMED agents, not a search over arbitrary structures.
2. **One latent of three, and three pairwise latents in the same block, are
   indistinguishable by any available evidence.** Groups are therefore canonicalised to
   MAXIMAL CLIQUES per owner (`maximal_cliques`, `canonical_groups`), which deletes exactly
   the undecidable refinements and keeps the decidable ones.
3. **The evidence arrives only when a partner intervenes on its own private variables.**
   `ma/env.py::_disclose_partner_responses` tells each agent which of its confounded pairs
   moved when a NAMED partner acted — never which variable was touched.

**Found while building it, and it reframes every earlier deterministic number:** the old
version-space belief prunes on the agent's OWN window columns only
(`cb/versionspace.py:310`), and a partner's private variable is not a window column. So a
partner's private experiment was invisible. **Altruism was not expressible in the
deterministic environment at all**, and the 0.937 headline from 26 August is a statement
about dividing shared work with no altruism component.

## 3. What is established, with the caveats attached

Deterministic environment, strict grading (every type claim required), scale-free graphs.

**Fixed policies, 3 agents x 2 private + 3 shared, budget 12** (`attr_probe.py` in the
session scratchpad, 40 episodes):

| policy | structure | attribution | identified |
|---|---|---|---|
| shared only | 0.864 | **0.000** | 0.000 |
| private only | 0.891 | **0.981** | 0.292 |
| probe then work | 0.926 | 0.907 | **0.658** |
| random | 0.861 | 0.407 | 0.275 |

Attribution evidence arrives ONLY from a partner's private experiment (0.000 for
shared-only, and that is not "rarely"). Either pure strategy lands near 0.29; the mixture
more than doubles it. That is a coordination problem in the strict sense: the group-optimal
behaviour is one no selfish agent has a reason to adopt.

**Learned policies, same configuration, 3 seeds x 3200 episodes** (`results/vs_attr/`):

| arm | identified | attribution | structure | share of budget on private probes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| learned s0/s1/s2 | 0.662 / 0.671 / 0.678 | 0.822 / 0.809 / 0.785 | ~0.894 | 0.56 / 0.56 / 0.46 |
| **probe_then_work** | **0.667 +/- 0.034** | **0.846** | 0.898 | 0.49 |
| greedy_uncertainty | 0.582 +/- 0.031 | 0.696 | 0.893 | 0.175 |
| random_vary | 0.340 +/- 0.033 | 0.487 | 0.886 | 0.359 |

All arms on IDENTICAL episodes, 150 each. **The learners MATCH the hand-designed reference**
(0.670 +/- 0.005 across seeds against 0.667 +/- 0.034) and both beat greedy by about 2.5
standard errors. They found it under a PER-AGENT reward, in which a private probe pays the
partners and not the prober.

**CAVEATS, and take them seriously:**

- Learned MATCHES `probe_then_work`; it does not beat it. Say "found a strategy as good as
  the one derived from theory, without being told it", not "beat the baseline".
- "Share of budget on private probes" is NOT a clean altruism measure. A private
  intervention also orients the agent's OWN private-incident edges, so it serves two
  purposes at once. The system-level claim (the group probes enough for everyone's
  attribution to settle) is safe; "individual agents behave altruistically" is not yet
  supported and needs an experiment that separates the two motives. Job 2 bears on this.

**Transfer, deterministic -> statistical, 4 agents x 1 private, budget 8, 60 episodes**
(`results/transfer/behaviour_4a.json`):

| environment | arm | identified | duplicate coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| deterministic | learned s0/s1/s2 | 0.988 / 0.967 / 0.954 | 0.041 / 0.076 / 0.072 |
| deterministic | greedy | 0.729 +/- 0.036 | 0.286 |
| deterministic | random | 0.633 +/- 0.036 | 0.476 |
| statistical | **transferred s0** | **0.171 +/- 0.035** | **0.116** |
| statistical | greedy | 0.229 +/- 0.037 | 0.447 |
| statistical | random | 0.208 +/- 0.034 | 0.504 |

**THE COORDINATION TRANSFERS AND THE PAYOFF DOES NOT.** The transferred policy divides the
work four times better than anything else in the statistical environment and still scores
worse than random. It did not become confused -- a confused policy drifts TOWARD random,
and this one stayed at 0.116 duplication. It executed its trained strategy faithfully, and
the strategy is wrong there.

**WHY, still open — and one plausible answer has already been REFUTED.** The first
hypothesis was that repetition buys statistical power: a repeat intervention is provably
worthless in the deterministic environment (pruning is idempotent), so the idealisation
teaches "touch everything once", and that prior would be inverted where more rows in one
regime means power. **Measured 27 August, 50 episodes per cell, and it is wrong** — dividing
the work is better in BOTH environments and the ordering is identical:

| arm | duplicate coverage | deterministic | statistical |
|---|---|---|---|
| divided | 0.235 | 1.000 | **0.245** |
| offset | 0.235 | 1.000 | **0.245** |
| random | 0.500 | 0.350 | 0.095 |
| converged | 0.750 | 0.130 | 0.085 |

A static, belief-blind rule that hands each agent a different shared variable scores 1.000
in the deterministic environment and is the BEST arm in the statistical one too. So
repetition is harmful in both, and a "belief sharpness" dial that rewards repeats is the
wrong fix. Do not build it.

The live lead is BUDGET ALLOCATION rather than pattern. The transferred policy spends 0.550
of its moves on its own private variable against greedy's 0.327, and touches only 0.69
distinct shared variables per agent against greedy's 0.91 — it relies on partners to cover
the shared surface. That is optimal in the deterministic environment (it scores 0.988) and
may not be where information is not free. **Untested. Do not repeat it as a finding.**

### Job 1 — scale attribution past three agents (highest value; nothing above 3 exists)

```bash
for n in 4 6; do for seed in 0 1; do
  .venv/bin/python scripts/ma_train.py --n_agents $n --private_size 2 --n_shared 3 \
    --budget $((n * 4)) --backend attributed --graph_model sf --claim_bar 1.0 \
    --reward_criterion claims --per_agent_reward --policy_arch gnn \
    --turn_order round_robin --episode_mix confounded --disclose_regime \
    --n_obs 60 --n_int 20 --observe_belief_channels --observe_partner_counts \
    --vary_only --train_episodes 4000 --eval_episodes 150 --seed $seed \
    --arm attr${n}a --out results/attr_scale/attr${n}a_s${seed}.json
done; done
```
Time a 32-episode run first: cost grows with window size and with the number of partners
(the attribution space is exponential in the partner count).

**What it decides:** whether the learned-vs-greedy gap survives more agents. At 3 agents it
is learned 0.670 vs greedy 0.582. If it closes at 6 the way the OLD target's gap did, that
is the contention effect — too many agents for three shared variables — and the fix is more
shared variables, not fewer agents. Report agents-per-shared-variable alongside.

### Job 2 — reward ablation, per-agent vs shared (the most interesting open question)

Identical to job 1 at `--n_agents 3 --private_size 2 --budget 12`, three seeds, with and
without `--per_agent_reward`.

**Why it matters more than it looks.** The agents found the altruistic-looking strategy —
about half their budget on private probes that pay only their PARTNERS — while being paid
only for their own window. If a shared reward is no better, the claim becomes "coordination
emerges without being paid for", which is much stronger than "we paid for coordination and
got it". If a shared reward is much better, the honest claim is the weaker one.

### Job 3 — private sets of three (tests the identifiability mechanism directly)

Same as job 1 with `--private_size 3` at 3 and 4 agents. Window size 6, the top of the
usable range — **time it before committing**.

**What it decides:** attribution needs a partner to probe its variables ONE AT A TIME,
because only a PARTIAL response refutes a single-clique hypothesis. More private variables
means more probes needed and more that can be distinguished. If attribution accuracy falls
with private-set size while structure holds, that isolates the mechanism.

### Job 4 — Erdos-Renyi vs scale-free, matched

Job 1's 3-agent configuration with `--graph_model er` against the existing `sf` results.

**Warning, measured:** dense ER windows at k=5 reach 2.7M candidates and 8 s/episode;
scale-free at the same size is 0.4 s. Time it, and if ER is unusable at private_size 2,
run it at private_size 1 and say so rather than dropping the arm.

**What it decides:** whether the attribution result is generator-specific. Everything
attributed so far is scale-free only, chosen because it produces twice the confounding
(0.45 of windows vs 0.23) and five times the three-variable groups.

### Reporting

For every job report identification, attribution accuracy, structure accuracy, and the

## 5. Traps — every one of these cost time already

- **`make_baselines` builds ALL baselines eagerly**, including `GreedyAgent`, which
  enumerates and refuses past window size 5 and raises `NotImplementedError` on any
  non-exact backend when CALLED. It has crashed two separate jobs. Construct the single
  baseline you need.
- **Hard-coded backend lists.** `ma/evaluate.py` listed the claim-scoring backends
  literally in two places, both missed when the attributed backend landed, so a 25-minute
  run died inside its own report. Fixed to use `CLAIM_BACKENDS` from `ma/env.py`. If you
  add a backend, grep for the literal before assuming you are done.
- **Baselines that count queries instead of moves.** Under turn-taking every policy is
  queried every round and the inactive agent's move is DISCARDED. `ProbeThenWorkAgent`
  counted queries, advanced three times per action, and scored 0.000 on the thing it was
  built to do. Read `env.own_counts`, which counts applied moves.
- **Underpowered comparisons.** Three separately reported "results" on 26 August were
  differences smaller than their own noise, at 30-40 episodes. Both evaluators already run
  every arm on IDENTICAL episode seeds, so compute the per-episode DIFFERENCE and its
  standard error rather than differencing two means. Do not report anything off fewer than
  150 episodes.
- **Whole-world changes when you meant one agent.** An experiment meant to isolate one
  agent's intervention mode set the mode for every agent, and could not answer its own
  question. Check that the thing you varied is the thing you meant to vary.
- **The budget must make the task winnable.** Under the strict grading, one round per agent
  leaves 25 of 40 episodes unsolvable by ANY assignment. Budget is `2 x n_agents`, and
  `scripts/vs_evaluate.py --optimal_rounds` will tell you the exact optimum for a new
  configuration. Check it before trusting a comparison at a new size.

## 7. Open design question, not yet decided

The attribution evidence is currently delivered by a bespoke hook
(`_disclose_partner_responses`). The cleaner formulation is the standard Joint Causal
Inference move: add a context column per partner, treat it as an ordinary variable, and let
the existing machinery do the work — same information, one rule instead of two, and it
would make the deterministic and statistical engines structurally identical. The student
approved building it as a SECOND backend alongside the current one, so that reverting is a
config flag and the two can be compared on identical episodes. Not started. The risk is
that it touches the enumeration core (§6).
