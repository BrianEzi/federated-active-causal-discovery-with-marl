the thesis. Supersedes section 5 of [`SESSION_STATE_2026_08_30.md`](SESSION_STATE_2026_08_30.md),
whose schedule rested on a fit rather than a measurement and on a launch path that had
three defects in it.

**The window.** Code freeze 31 Aug morning. Experiments to 2 Sep. Write-up to 7 Sep 15:00.
From now (30 Aug, ~16:00) that is roughly **74 hours of wall clock** for compute.

**The machine.** 10 cores — 6 performance, 4 efficiency — and 24 GB. Runs are single
threaded (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`), so throughput comes from running many at once. The
efficiency cores are far slower than the performance cores, so 8 workers is **not** 8x;
`scripts/preflight_runs.py throughput` measures the real factor and every wall-clock figure
below is core-hours divided by *that*, not by the worker count.

## 3. The finding that came out of the feasibility gate

The gate exists to check that beta is normalised. It is — every cell's budget admits the
ceiling arm at 1.000 — but the comparison against the old window ladder is the story:

| shape | ladder budget | beta=1.5 budget | greedy, ladder | greedy, normalised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k=4  | 8  | 19 | 0.753 | — |
| k=12 | 16 | 50 | 0.180 | 0.880 – 0.960 |
| k=20 | 20 | 75 | 0.033 | — |
| k=30 | **15** | 98 | **0.000** | **0.760** |

**The ladder gave k=30 a smaller budget than k=20.** So the headline scaling curve — that
identification collapses as the window grows — was in substantial part a budget-starvation
curve wearing a window-size costume. At a budget normalised to the required cover, greedy
at k=30 goes from 0.000 to 0.760.

This is the quantified version of what `a2492c9` found, and it retires a concern that would
otherwise have reshaped this plan: **k=30 is a live cell, not a floor**, and the poor
entropy convergence at w20/w30 in the old runs is at least partly a task with no reward
signal in it rather than a policy that needs more episodes. Episode count stays at 4,000
for now, and section 6 says what would change that.

## 4. Measured cost

From `scripts/calibrate_sweep.py`, which fits a slope between two run lengths so the
fixed warm-up cancels. Measured on an **untrained** policy, so episodes run to the budget;
a policy that learns ends them early, and the estimate is therefore biased *high*.

| cell | k | n | budget | s/episode | per run (4,000 eps + 200-ep eval) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k04s50n04b150 | 4 | 4 | 19 | 0.064 | 4.6 m |
| k08s50n04b150 | 8 | 4 | 35 | 0.184 | 13.5 m |
| k12s50n04b100 | 12 | 4 | 34 | 0.441 | 31.9 m |
| k12s50n04b150 | 12 | 4 | 50 | 0.479 | 34.1 m |
| k12s50n04b200 | 12 | 4 | 67 | 0.793 | 56.1 m |
| k12s50n04b500 | 12 | 4 | 166 | 1.771 | 123.6 m |

Cost is **linear in the budget at fixed k** (0.00958 s/round at k=12 against 0.01067 at
3.3x the budget) and close to **linear in k** on top of that, so

    seconds per episode  ~=  0.0008 * budget * k

which is the model the remaining cells are checked against as they land.

## 5. The order of work

Ranked by what the thesis cannot be written without. Each tier is independently
publishable, so a tier that does not finish costs a chapter and not the thesis.

**Tier A — the spine.**
1. **Oracle sweep**, 20 cells x 3 seeds. Delivers the k, sigma, n and beta marginals and
   the one interaction block, every cell against a ceiling and a coordinated control.
2. **Rung 1 exact**, k=5 / 3 agents. The only regime where claims are checkable against an
   exact optimum rather than a bound, and the only one where the `attributed` backend runs.

**Tier B — realism.** The sampled sweep: the same cells with `--vs_evidence sampled`,
retrained rather than transferred (an oracle-trained policy loses under sampling; the
mechanism is the repeat rate, and it is a correct rule for the wrong regime).

**Tier C — as time allows,** in this order: C3 solo with n x episodes (answers Mirco
directly: does decentralisation cost data or capability); FedAvg at E in {1, 4}; solo +
`normalise_returns` at 6 and 8 agents, which are currently unquotable because those nets
never trained; E5 Erdos-Renyi.

## 6. What would change this plan

- **Convergence.** At 4,000 episodes the old runs ended at roughly half their initial
  entropy. Section 3 argues much of that was budget starvation, but it does not settle it.
  The check is free: the checkpoint schedule keeps update 250 inside any longer run, so
  "at 4,000 episodes" stays readable from a 10,000-episode run. **Read the MI gate and the
  entropy curve off the first finished cells and extend only if they are still climbing.**
- **The n axis.** If per-episode cost turns out to scale with agent count on top of the
  budget, the n=15 cell alone could exceed 7 hours per seed. Calibration will say; if it
  does, n=15 drops to one seed rather than the whole axis being cut.
- **`greedy_partitioned` at 0.450** against uncoordinated greedy's 0.960 at the baseline
  cell. The positional partition *hurts*, because it hands each agent 1/n of a shared
  surface it needs covered in full. That is a legitimate control, but it is a **weak** one,
  and it should not be read as "coordination does not help" without understanding it first.

## 7. The rule, unchanged

Every number is quoted with three things or not at all: **the MI gate, the evidence mode,
and the evaluation policy (argmax or sampled).** Every wrong claim on this project has come
from one of the three being left implicit.
