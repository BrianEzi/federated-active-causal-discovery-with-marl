Confounded by Design

Two agents share a causal system. Neither may see the other's private variables, so each one's view is confounded by construction — and the only way to break that confounding is for your partner to spend a move helping you. This is where that experiment stands, including what was withdrawn from it this morning.

Three things to take from this briefing.

The single-agent half is settled: a learned policy matches or beats the myopic information-gain oracle across the whole budget grid at five variables, and most decisively where the experiment budget is tight.

The two-agent half had a measurement fault, found and fixed this morning. The reported success metric could not be earned at all on confounded episodes — the case the entire design exists for — so every two-agent number produced before today is an unconfounded-episodes-only figure wearing a general label. All of them are withdrawn, and the retraction is set out in full below rather than buried.

The re-measurement is done, and it holds up. GATE 3 — the gate that establishes coordination is both necessary and available, and the only one that scores confounded episodes exclusively — passes again under the corrected criterion with a larger sample and a wider margin than before. And on the corrected metric a learned policy beats its own random floor on every seed run so far, with non-overlapping intervals, using roughly half the moves. That is the simplest two-agent case working — on a small number of seeds, so it is a sanity check rather than a sized effect. Finished so far: 8 with the regime bit and 3 without. A twenty-task cluster array covers the zero-cost control.

The problem, stated precisely

What the agents can see, what they can do, and what counts as success

One linear Gaussian system, five variables, two agents. Agent A owns one private variable, agent B owns another, and three are shared. Each agent observes its own private variable and all shared ones. It never observes its partner's private variable, there is no central server, and training is independent PPO — each agent learns from its own trajectory, with no centralised critic.

The federation boundary is what creates the science. Because a partner's private variable is invisible, any causal path running through it appears inside your window as latent confounding between two shared variables — a correlation with no visible cause. We proved, and then verified exhaustively over the graph space, that this confounding is confined to the shared set: no other pair of variables can be confounded. That result is what makes the belief tractable, because the agent's posterior ranges over (window DAG, confounding assignment) pairs and the assignment only has to enumerate shared pairs — 33 = 27 assignments, of which 2 admit no acyclic completion, leaving 25.

Each round an agent picks a variable and a mode. It can vary it — a randomised hard intervention drawn from a Gaussian — or clamp it, holding it at a constant. Clamping is the interesting one: holding your own private variable constant is what lets your partner tell a genuine shared edge apart from confounding through you. It is the only altruistic act in the game, and it costs you, because a variable held constant teaches you nothing about what drives it.

Why the coordination problem is about timing, not willingness

An agent that clamps its private variable every round never learns that variable's own parents — and those edges are part of its own success criterion. Traced directly: agent A rises 0.368 → 0.814 while B, clamping every round, stays at 0.04 and never identifies. Pure altruism is strictly dominated. What has to be discovered is a mix — clamp on some rounds, experiment on others — which is why the reference ceiling in GATE 3 is a mixed policy rather than an always-clamping one.

Success is a three-part criterion, and all three parts must hold. Each agent's posterior must place at least 0.7 of its mass on its credit set: every edge touching a private variable exactly right, and Markov-equivalent on the rest — because orientation inside an equivalence class is not identifiable from data and demanding it would be measuring floating-point tie-breaks. Then the union of the two agents' answers must be acyclic, and Markov-equivalent to the true global graph. The acyclicity check is not redundant: two agents can orient a shared edge differently within the same equivalence class and union into a cycle, which is exactly the failure the relaxation admits.

Single agent: the settled result

Learned policy against the myopic information-gain oracle

The opponent is a greedy oracle that picks the single highest-expected-information-gain intervention at every step, using the exact posterior. It is myopic by construction: a one-step optimum, not a sequential one. That is precisely why beating it is possible — optimal sequential design is not greedy design chained together, and expected information gain is not adaptive submodular, so no (1−1/e) guarantee protects it.

We report gap_closed: 0 means the learned policy matched random, 1 means it matched the oracle, above 1 means it beat the oracle. Three seeds per cell, 150 evaluation episodes each.

The strongest cell is five variables at budget 2, where all three seeds land at or above 1.2 — the learned policy solves 0.856 of episodes against the oracle's 0.833 and random's 0.460. Across the full grid the median clears 1.0 in ten of fourteen cells, and every cell at five variables clears it. All four shortfalls are at seven variables: three of them where only 100 observational samples are available, bottoming out at 0.529, and one marginal miss at 0.973. The reading is that the learned advantage is real at five variables and thins as the space grows — which is exactly the boundary the scaling work below has to push on.

The caveat that belongs with these numbers

GATE 1 does not pass in the single-agent setting at five or seven variables. The observational-only identification rate is below its predicted target in every cell — 0.040 against 0.089 at five variables, 0.000 at the scarce-data settings — and the gate is deliberately two-sided. A rate above target would be a leak, meaning the task is solvable without intervening, and would invalidate everything built on it. A rate below target is the opposite failure: the posterior cannot reach the 0.7 identification threshold even on graphs that are uniquely identifiable, so the task is harder than intended, not easier. The comparison between policies stands, because all three face the same environment; what does not stand is any claim about absolute identification rates. Recovering the gate means either more data or a threshold derived per setting rather than inherited — part of the parameter audit below.

What the belief actually computes

And the chain of independent checks under it

The agent carries an exact Bayesian posterior, not a learned estimator. Structure scores use the BGe marginal likelihood (Geiger & Heckerman, 2002), which integrates the parameters out and is score-equivalent by construction — members of one equivalence class score identically, which is a property we test rather than hope for. Interventional data enters by the Cooper & Yoo (1999) rule: a hard-intervened variable contributes no likelihood term for itself, but its realised value remains valid evidence for its children.

Enumerating DAGs stops being possible at about six variables, so the posterior is computed by a subset dynamic program over sink orderings — Robinson's recurrence — carried out in signed log space. The sign matters: the inclusion–exclusion terms alternate, and per-node rescaling cannot work, which we established the expensive way. That moves the reachable size from six variables to roughly nine.

Confounding is handled by marginalising the orientation of each confounding edge rather than fixing it with a tie-break, and that was not cosmetic tidying. The previous code oriented each confounding edge along the DAG's topological order — but a DAG has many topological orders, and for two shared variables that are incomparable in the DAG the choice came down to which one had the lower index. The two orientations score differently under BGe, so the hypothesis being evaluated depended on node numbering. Making the orientation part of the hypothesis and integrating it out removes the arbitrary choice, and has the side benefit that acyclicity comes for free because the dynamic program only emits DAGs. Finally, the oracle needs posterior samples, so a Metropolis–Hastings sampler was replaced with exact sampling by source-layer decomposition (Talvitie, Vuoksenmaa & Koivisto, UAI 2019), which sidesteps the signed terms entirely.

The independent checks

  • Subset DP against frozen enumeration — agreement to 1e−10, against a fixture generated by the previous generation of the code, so the check is not circular.
  • Exact sampler against the DP's partition function — agreement to 1e−13, through a completely different derivation.
  • Oracle information loss — the exact sampler loses 0.0018 nats against an exact reference where Metropolis–Hastings lost 0.1116: a factor of 61×. Doubling the draws changes nothing, which is the check that the remaining error is not a sample-size problem.
  • Test suite — 534 tests, all passing, against the revision that produced these numbers.

GATE 2 is open, and it is the honest weak point

At two agents the myopic oracle is no better than random. The live hypothesis is collision: both greedy agents compute the same objective over overlapping authority, pick the same target, and waste the round — measured at 0.352 of rounds against random's 0.227. If that holds, the finding is that myopic design does not survive decentralisation, which is a result worth having rather than an obstacle. What it is not is a reason to keep quoting the oracle as a reference; either GATE 2 is resolved, or greedy is formally retired as a benchmark and random becomes the reference.

What was withdrawn this morning

One confusion, four measurement bugs, and the guard now standing over it

A hypothesis in this belief is a pair: a DAG H, and a set P of confounded shared pairs whose edges are present in H. So the agent's causal claim is H minus P — never H itself. The reported metric compared H against the true causal graph. On a confounded episode the truth therefore matched only under the empty assignment: the one hypothesis that refuses to model the confounding at all.

The consequence is stark. Reported success on confounded episodes was exactly 0.000 — not low, not noisy, structurally unreachable — against roughly 0.59 on unconfounded ones. Confounded episodes are the entire point of the two-agent design, so every headline number we had was silently conditioned on the cases that do not need coordination.

"The myopic oracle never clamps." An artefact: the baseline's action list was hardcoded to vary-only, so it could not have clamped. The real claim is weaker and more interesting — myopic expected information gain is indifferent between the two modes, so the tie-break decides, measured at 0.526.

The GATE 3 headroom of +0.393, measured against a random baseline whose two agents shared an RNG stream and therefore collided on 78% of rounds against an expected 19%. Superseded twice: to +0.184 once the baseline was desynchronised, and now to +0.237 on the corrected criterion with four times the sample.

That is four measurement bugs in two days, and three of them are the same confusion appearing in three different places — in the reward, in the reported metric, and in the identification criterion. The pattern is worth naming because it generalises past this project.

The guard that now exists

A metric can be perfectly well-formed and still be structurally unearnable, and the two look identical in a results table. The whole suite passed, green, while the reported two-agent metric could not be earned at all on confounded episodes — because every test asked whether the metric computed the right number, and none asked whether that number was reachable in the first place.

Every metric now carries an explicit reachability case, per regime it claims to cover: a test that constructs the best achievable belief in that regime and asserts the metric can be earned. tests/ma/test_metric_reachability.py.

The same discipline was applied backwards, and it paid. GATE 3 had been recorded at 01:12 this morning, before the fix, and it is the one gate that scores confounded episodes exclusively — exactly the regime the broken criterion could not credit. Rather than reason about which way the error ran, it was re-measured from scratch, on 2000 attempted episodes instead of 400.

It passes, and the margin got wider: headroom +0.237 on 169 confounded episodes, against +0.184 on 38 before. That is the opposite of what I expected. The old criterion demanded the exact true DAG together with the exact confounding set, which is strictly harder than the criterion we actually specified, and it was penalising the arm that can clamp more than the arm that cannot. Reasoning would have got the sign wrong; measuring did not.

Two agents: provisional

all 6 corrected runs finished — three seeds per arm, 2000 training episodes, budget 8

These are the first two-agent numbers whose value on a confounded episode is not structurally zero. Treat them as a sanity check on the corrected pipeline rather than a result: a handful of seeds is enough to see whether learning happens, and not enough to size the effect. Finished so far: 8 with the regime bit and 3 without. A twenty-task cluster array covers the zero-cost control.

The comparison being re-run is the regime bit — whether telling an agent which data-collection regime each sample came from is what makes coordination learnable. Today's runs sharpened that question rather than answering it. Disclosure turns out to move the belief, not just the policy: the no-bit arm's own random floor sits near 0.03, against 0.19 for the with-bit arm, on the same policy class and the same budget. So most of the bit's effect happens upstream of any learning, by making the confounding claim identifiable at all.

Two consequences. First, cross-arm comparisons of the learned rate are meaningless, and each arm is scored against its own random floor throughout. Second, the with-bit / no-bit contrast is not the clean test of learnability it was being read as, because it confounds a belief effect with a learning effect — which is what the zero-cost control is for.

with regime bit: the learned policy separates from random on 7 of 8 seeds (0.467 vs 0.200, 0.327 vs 0.160, 0.500 vs 0.200, 0.320 vs 0.213, 0.520 vs 0.200, 0.400 vs 0.193, 0.373 vs 0.153, 0.467 vs 0.160). no regime bit: the learned policy separates from random on 0 of 3 seeds (0.000 vs 0.080, 0.000 vs 0.027, 0.000 vs 0.047) — though 3 of those 3 seeds stopped acting altogether, so they are collapses under the step cost rather than failures to learn. Independently of any learning, a random policy that may clamp scores 0.148 on average against 0.056 for one that never does — the same coordination effect GATE 3 isolates, reproduced inside the training evaluation.

One parameter is doing more work than it should

The per-step cost is set to 0.05 and has never been swept. At roughly 7.7 steps per episode that is a charge of about 0.39 against a success reward of 1 — so on every run we have measured, a policy performing at random's level has negative expected value against 0.000 for doing nothing. Passing is optimal, and an arm that collapses into passing is behaving correctly rather than failing to learn.

The control has now run, and it splits the question cleanly. With the cost set to zero and nothing else changed, the no-bit arm stops collapsing: it takes 8.4 steps per episode against 0.0, and final policy entropy is 1.93 against 0.02. So the collapse really was the reward design.

But it still does not learn anything. Success is 0.047 against its own random floor of 0.053 — indistinguishable. Acting freely and exploring fully, an agent without the regime bit cannot beat random. That is the answer we needed: the step cost explains the collapse, and it does not explain away the bit. Disclosure is doing real work, because without it the belief cannot separate confounding from a genuine shared edge, and no policy can recover what the belief cannot represent. (2 seeds.)

This is the clearest case of a wider problem: several parameters are asserted rather than measured, and that audit is scheduled below.
