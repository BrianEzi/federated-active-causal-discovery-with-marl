What the Agents Recover

Eight episodes from a trained pair, drawn out in full: the graph that generated the data, what each agent concluded from its own restricted view, and the moves it spent getting there.

Across 120 unseen episodes this policy pair solved 44% — 53% of the confounded ones and 43% of the unconfounded ones. It spent 6.6 moves per episode, and 77% of those moves were clamps.

Each agent sees its own private variable and the three shared ones. It never sees its partner's private variable — drawn as a dotted circle. Because a causal path can run through that invisible variable, an agent can be looking at two shared variables that move together with no visible cause between them. Telling that apart from a genuine edge is the entire problem, and the only way to do it is for the partner to spend a move clamping the hidden variable, which switches the pathway off.

How to read "credit mass"

An agent does not output one graph — it holds a probability distribution over all of them. The bar is how much of that probability sits on answers we accept as correct: every edge touching a private variable exactly right, and the rest right up to Markov equivalence, since some orientations are provably indistinguishable from any amount of data. It has to clear 0.70, and both agents have to clear it, and their answers have to union into an acyclic graph matching the truth. The picture shows the single most likely hypothesis; the bar shows how confident the agent is across all of them.

Reading the move strips

v = vary (a randomised intervention, for orienting edges), c = clamp (hold the variable at a constant, which switches off a confounding pathway for the partner), — = passed. Clamping your own private variable does nothing for you — it is the move that helps the other agent, and it costs you information about what drives that variable. Nothing in the reward names this behaviour; it has to be discovered from a shared scalar reward alone.
